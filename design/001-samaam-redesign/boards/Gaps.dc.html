Gap matrix

Where the regulation is silent, and what its silence costs.

Each gap rests on a documented absence of text, not on an assumption.

GAP-01Contrast thresholds exist. The gap moved to whether they bind.
The 2021 Ministry of Health protocol states the eGFR < 30 threshold, the metformin categories and the risk bands, but it is practice guidance. No text was found making it binding as the dose levels are.
Samaam cites a Saudi national protocol, and never presents it as a prohibition. It holds until an authorised person decides.
A decision linking the Ministry's radiology protocols to enforcement and accountability, as Royal Decree 60057 did for the reference levels.
GAP-02Liability when a tired technologist accepts an automated recommendation
SFDA requires studying human-computer interaction (MDS-G010) and human review safeguards (MDS-G027). No text says who carries responsibility when harm occurs.
The strongest gap in the project: a documented legislative void.
A text dividing responsibility between manufacturer, facility, technologist and consultant, with a documented digital signature before any safety alert is overridden.
GAP-03Reference levels bind, with no real-time enforcement
Royal Decree 60057 and Health Council Resolution 3/88 bind providers. MDS-G008 requires automatic dose recording and periodic reports to SFDA by email.
Enforcement is retrospective. It finds the breach after the patient was exposed.
Require radiology systems to check the reference level before imaging starts, not after. This is what Samaam provides.
GAP-04No cumulative dose ceiling for oncology patients
Reference levels are set per examination. No Saudi text sets a cumulative limit for patients imaged repeatedly in oncology follow-up.
Justification is applied scan by scan and does not see the patient's whole journey.
Require cumulative dose tracking across facilities in the unified health record.
GAP-05Device control is regulated more than clinical accountability
SFDA Rule 10 places software affecting radiology devices in class C. No text requires a physician's confirmation before kVp, mAs or contrast dose change, or records liability when alerts are ignored.
Samaam's registration path is clear. The operational governance layer is what is missing.
A regulation requiring every override to be recorded and tied to the identity of the authoriser.
GAP-06Data law is not medical-error law
The PDPL implementing regulation covers transparency of automated decisions. It does not say whether clinical judgement may be delegated to an automated system, or who pays compensation.
Relying on the PDPL alone to justify a medical system's safety is a method error.
An explicit link between the data protection law and the health professions regulations.
